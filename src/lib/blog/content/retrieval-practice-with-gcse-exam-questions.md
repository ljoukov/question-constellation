## Recall is a start

Many students turn retrieval practice into quick definitions. That can help, especially for one-mark recall. Longer GCSE answers need another layer.

A student may remember the word mitochondria and still fail to explain why more mitochondria matter in a cell. A complete answer needs to connect mitochondria, respiration, energy release and the process being supported.

## Exam retrieval should include structure

Question Constellation asks the student to produce the whole answer before seeing feedback. The student is asking two questions at once: what facts do I know, and which of them does this prompt require?

That makes retrieval more exam-shaped. The prompt is a real question, the checklist is tied to marks, and the next question asks for transfer rather than a repeat of the same sentence.

## A simple revision routine

Pick one question. Write the answer without notes. Check the marking points. Rewrite the complete answer with the missing points included. Then open a related question.

- Attempt first.
- Check the marking points.
- Improve the whole answer.
- Try a related question.

The routine is short, but it avoids the trap of reading a model answer and feeling fluent without proving recall.

## Why this matters near the exam

In the final weeks, many students reread notes because it feels calm. Exam-shaped retrieval is less comfortable, but it gives better information. It shows what the student can produce and which marking point needs more work.
